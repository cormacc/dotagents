Measured duplication across the 7 packaged personas [2026-08-08]: there is no literal copy-paste -- no identical line of 40 characters or more appears in two persona files. What recurs is the same *concept*, spelled differently each time, which is worse: the wording drifts silently. Counts by file: read-only 5 (scout, researcher, worker, reviewer, advisor), artifact/report-path convention 5 (scout, researcher, reviewer, advisor, visual-tester), no-delegation 4 (scout, researcher, advisor, visual-tester), verify-claims-against-source 3 (worker, reviewer, advisor), stay-scoped 3 (scout, researcher, advisor), do-not-manufacture-findings 2 (reviewer, advisor).

Design consideration found while measuring: no-delegation is already enforced mechanically, since the CLI checks the `spawns:` allow-list before any ledger or pane mutation. Its prose restatement in 4 personas is belt-and-braces rather than the guarantee. That suggests the first question is not "what composition mechanism?" but "which of these belongs in the injected wrapper (`cli.clj/prompt-text`, line 192, which already carries the delegation, identity, publication and retro instructions), which belongs in a shared fragment, and which is genuinely per-persona?" A mechanism chosen before that split is settled will encode the wrong boundary.

Option space, not yet decided: move universal rules into `prompt-text`; a `traits:`/`extends:` frontmatter key resolving named fragments through the same project > home > packaged chain personas already use; an include directive in the persona body; or convention plus `--prompt-extra` with no code change at all. Note `core.clj/parse-frontmatter` (line 32) already ignores unknown keys, so a new key is additive rather than breaking, and the existing list-frontmatter precedent is bare space-separated words (`spawns: scout researcher`).

*EDIT* [2026-08-08] Originally filed as "evaluate whether herdr-orch could/should leverage ai-behaviors" (https://github.com/xificurC/ai-behaviors), proposing hashtag behaviours at spawn time or `:behaviours [...]` in persona frontmatter. ** TODO herdr-orch: serialise publish against close and continue :herdr_orch:
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: b96cb5da-ebc1-4e04-aeed-af9531ffab18
:CREATED: [2026-08-07 Fri 23:18]
:END:
:LOGBOOK:

`publish` is not serialised against `close`/`continue`. The settle-wait rechecks added during the append-only stream work narrow the race but cannot close it: `publish` creates its item file through `Files.createLink`, independently of `ledger/update!`, so an item can be created after the lifecycle verb's recheck and before `herdr/close!` records `:closed-at`, or before a `continue` successor entry is written. Consequences: `close` can take a pane and record `:closed-at` with an unconsumed item on the round; `continue` can supersede a round while a publication that passed the old check is still committing.

Not a regression from the stream change -- the pre-change design carried a comparable publish-versus-close race -- and deliberately left open there, because a fix needs interprocess exclusion shared by `publish` and the lifecycle verbs (a lock file, or an equivalent atomic state transition), which was larger than that record's scope. A final check after closing is too late: closure is irreversible.

Identified and reproduced by the third closeout review of `43e7c65c`; full analysis in that review's artifact, and the accepted-risk entry is in `design/log/2026-08-07-herdr-orch-flexible-lifecycle-append-onl.org` under `** Risks`. Relevant symbols as of that work: `close-mutation!` and `continue!` in `skills/herdr-orch/scripts/src/herdr_orch/cli.clj`, plus `assert-publishable!` and the `createLink` publication path.

** TODO [#B] herdr-orch: eliminate delegation-driven focus changes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 97863e4a-3e91-470b-ad53-13857ef1201c
:CREATED: [2026-08-10 Mon 20:09]
:END:
:LOGBOOK:
- Created [2026-08-10 Mon 20:09]

Observed friction: a root spawn from a workspace other than the active one steals focus across workspaces. =cli.clj= passes =:focus= into =herdr/tab-create!=, which focuses the new tab in the caller's workspace regardless of where the operator actually is. Unattended delegation makes it worse -- every root spawn in a multi-child run yanks the operator once.

Proposal (user, 2026-08-10): eliminate delegation-driven focus entirely rather than gate it on the active workspace. Tab creation and the herdr agents panel already signal that a subagent exists, so the focus rules add machinery around a subjective preference.

Why elimination beats gating, in order of weight:
- Gating breaks the invariant that currently makes focus safe. =SKILL.md= states "Focus is only ever set, never read to decide anything." Gating on the active workspace requires reading workspace state to decide, and that read is racy: the operator can switch workspaces between the check and the =tab create= call. It also adds a third conditional axis on top of the existing depth gate and the flag > configured > default precedence.
- Elimination deletes a bug class rather than guarding it. =SKILL.md= already records that the depth gate is load-bearing "since =publish= makes every delegating child close its grandchildren, so an ungated hook would end every nested delegation in a focus steal". That is an admission that focus-on-close is a footgun needing a guard; with no focus there is nothing to guard. The depth gate remains for placement, where depth is a genuine distinction.
- The close-side focus return is arguably worse than the spawn-side one. If the operator never left, returning focus is a no-op; if they deliberately moved away, it pulls them back. It mainly exists to undo the other half of the feature.

Removal surface: =core/resolve-focus=; the =--focus= / =--no-focus= flags including their mutual-exclusion check, their =boolean-flags= entries, and their usage text; =:defaults :focus= in =subagents/config.edn= and the comment justifying it; the =:focus= argument threaded into =tab-create!= and =split!= at spawn; the best-effort focus return on root =close=, =collect --close=, =close --settled=, and =orphans --close=; the =:focus= field in ledger entries; and the Focus bullet in =SKILL.md= plus its counterparts in =README.org=, =scripts/README.md=, and =scripts/docs/contract.md=.

Explicitly out of scope: the raw passthrough verbs =oh tab create --focus=, =oh ws focus=, =oh tab focus=, and =oh agent focus=. Those are operator actions, not delegation side effects, and upstream Herdr owns their semantics.

Acceptance criteria:
- No delegation path sets focus: spawn, close, =collect --close=, both sweeps. Verified by spawning from a non-active workspace and observing that focus does not move.
- Confirm before removing, rather than assuming: that the herdr agents panel does surface a newly created subagent, since that is the premise the removal rests on. If it does not, elimination trades a focus steal for a lost child and the proposal needs revisiting.
- =:defaults= keeps =:placement= and is validated as a closed map with the =:focus= key gone; a config still declaring =:focus= fails with its file path rather than being silently ignored.
- Existing focus assertions in =cli_test.clj=, =core_test.clj=, and =herdr_test.clj= are removed or inverted to assert that no focus call is made, rather than deleted wholesale leaving the behaviour unpinned.
- =bb test= and =ot doctor= stay clean.

Not measured: how often auto-focus is actually wanted. The evidence is one session of unattended multi-child delegation where it was pure noise, plus the reported cross-workspace steal.
