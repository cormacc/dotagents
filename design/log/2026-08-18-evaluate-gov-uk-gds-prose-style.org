* Intent

Decide which prose-style skill or skills `home/AGENTS.md` mandates, on benchmark evidence. Round 1 compared the govuk-style gist and SimpleEnglish against the incumbent vendored `asd-ste100` and recommended rejection; the user superseded that recommendation with a pivot. The pivoted work authors two owned skills -- `technical-prose`, a runtime-lean refactor of the incumbent, and `simple-prose`, adapted from the GOV.UK references -- and decides the mandate from a round 2 benchmark over four candidates on samples the incumbent mandate never touched.

* Summary

*Level:* MVP · *Tests:* none · *Docs:* inline (this record plus its same-stem artefacts folder)

** Scope

*** In scope
- A fixed rubric, three fixed reference samples, and one snapshot of each candidate skill under `design/log/2026-08-18-evaluate-gov-uk-gds-prose-style/`.
- Three isolated evaluation runs, one for each candidate: the govuk-style gist, SimpleEnglish, and the incumbent `asd-ste100`.
- A skill-artifact audit that answers whether each candidate corrects the runtime-irrelevant-context defect in the incumbent.
- A comparison report and an adopt / reject / split recommendation in this record.
- An owned `technical-prose` skill: a refactor of the vendored `asd-ste100` whose body keeps only write-time rules and moves the rest to a secondary references file.
- An owned `simple-prose` skill: authored from the two gov.uk pages with the gist as prior art, carrying an explicit meaning-preservation rule.
- A round 2 benchmark over four candidates -- `asd-ste100`, `technical-prose`, `simple-prose`, and the govuk-style gist snapshot -- on samples the incumbent mandate never touched, under the round 1 rubric discipline.
- The mandate rewire, gated on the confirmed round 2 decision: update the `home/AGENTS.md` mandate and the `skills/README.org` inventory.

*** Out of scope
- Rewrites of existing repository prose after a mandate change. The new mandate applies to prose written after adoption.
- Edits to the vendored `skills/asd-ste100` body.
- Reproduction of the ASD approved-word dictionary in any owned artefact.
- Blinded scoring. The effort line is MVP; the rubric is fixed before scoring, and that is the rigour bar.
- SimpleEnglish as a round 2 candidate. It never beat the incumbent, and its Rule 1.14 mandates American spelling against the repository's British English rule.
- A single dual-voice skill in this record. Two discrete self-contained skills compare more simply; a merge is a post-round-2 open question.

** Decisions
- Confirmed [2026-08-19]: no prose skill is mandated. `home/AGENTS.md` carries the instruction directly :: across two model tiers the measured quality margin for any skill was at most 1 point, thin and unreplicated, while the measured token cost was 1.8x to 3.4x the instruction alone. The cost is certain and recurring; the benefit is not established. Every candidate prose skill is discarded, including the two authored during this work, and the vendored incumbent is deleted.
  Known counter-evidence, accepted deliberately: round 3 found the instruction losing 1 point to the skills on strict text on the weaker local model, where round 2 tied. That is one sample, one run, and it is the only signal pointing the other way. If a weaker model is later adopted for prose work, re-measure before assuming this decision still holds.
  Rejected:
  - Split by prose class with `simple-prose` for explanatory text: it was the cheapest adoptable skill and matched the incumbent on quality, but its margin over the instruction was the same thin single point, so it does not clear the bar its own evaluation set.
  - Retain the incumbent: it tied on quality, cost the most of any condition, and still carried the runtime-context defect this work set out to fix.
- Retain the full experimental setup in the design tree, against usual closure practice [2026-08-19] :: `org-plan` closure would prune this material. It is kept deliberately, so a future exercise of the same kind can reuse the rubric, the samples, the harness notes, and the candidate skills rather than rebuild them, and so the decision above stays auditable against its own evidence. The retained `candidates/technical-prose/` is an MIT derivative of the deleted incumbent; its `LICENSE` and `references/provenance.md` travel with it for that reason.
- Author an owned GDS skill if the evaluation favours GDS :: an owned skill is lean by construction and repository conventions apply.
  Rejected:
  - Vendor the fofr gist: a gist has no `skills/` structure, so the `skills-lock.json` mechanism cannot track it, and the no-local-edits rule for vendored skills blocks the exact fix this task wants.
  - Defer the choice until after the evaluation: the tradeoffs are already known and do not depend on the scores.
- Fresh session for each candidate :: each session loads only one candidate, so the incumbent cannot contaminate the other runs.
  Rejected:
  - Single-session side-by-side audit: cheaper, but the incumbent is already in the session's mandated context.
- Contamination control by temporary mandate removal :: remove the `home/AGENTS.md` § ASD-STE100 block for the duration of the evaluation runs and restore it with git afterwards. Prompt-level overrides were rejected as less reliable than removal of the instruction itself.
- Separate rubric dimensions for each prose class :: an STE-derived rubric structurally favours STE candidates, so strict-text and explanatory-prose scores stay separate and the decision weighs them separately. This is what makes a split mandate decidable.
- Round 1 recommendation, superseded [2026-08-19] by the pivot decision below: reject both candidates and retain the incumbent `asd-ste100` mandate :: the incumbent won or tied every output dimension (strict 4, explanatory 8 of 10). govuk-style dropped content in two of three samples (a scope qualifier and a whole `** Shipped` item), which caps its scores under the rubric's meaning-preservation rule. SimpleEnglish preserved meaning but scored below the incumbent on explanatory prose, mandates American spelling against the repository's British English rule, and costs ~35% more context words. No split is supported: the would-be explanatory winner is the candidate that dropped content. Full comparison and caveats (one run per candidate; all runs on claude-opus-5): `design/log/2026-08-18-evaluate-gov-uk-gds-prose-style/report.md`. The user superseded this recommendation with the pivot rather than confirming it.
- Pivot [2026-08-19]: author two owned skills and decide the mandate in a round 2 benchmark :: round 1 showed the incumbent's rules win on output quality while its body carries ~400 runtime-irrelevant words, and showed the govuk voice produces the leanest artefact and the best README rewrite while its two content drops remain unattributed (one run per candidate). Owned skills correct the artefact defects on both sides, and a four-candidate round 2 (`asd-ste100`, `technical-prose`, `simple-prose`, the govuk-style gist snapshot) makes the drops testable: a repeat drop in the gist run alongside a clean `simple-prose` run attributes the defect to the gist; two clean runs point to run variance.
  Rejected:
  - Accept the round 1 rejection and stop: leaves the incumbent's measured ~16% runtime-irrelevant body cost uncorrected. That premise was later corrected -- see the artefact-audit correction below -- but the pivot stands on the output-quality and voice grounds in the entry above.
- Round 1 artefact audit over-counted the incumbent's runtime-irrelevant body [corrected 2026-08-19] :: the audit classified whole sections as provenance and reported ~400 runtime-irrelevant words (~16%). The `technical-prose` refactor found that ~307 of those words are write-time rules embedded in `## Source and Scope` and `## Boundaries`: the no-dictionary-reproduction statement, the plainest-word principle, the word-by-word escape hatch for exact ASD wording, and two will-not bullets that govern what an agent may claim. They stayed in the body. The genuinely extractable figure is ~93 words, measured as the loaded body falling from 2,435 to 2,342 words at equivalent rules. Classify at rule granularity, not section granularity, in any further artefact audit.
  - Keep SimpleEnglish in round 2: it never beat the incumbent, mandates American spelling against the British English rule, and costs ~35% more context words.
  - Author one dual-voice skill first: two discrete self-contained skills compare more simply; a merge stays available after round 2 (see `* Open questions`).
- First `technical-prose` version is a refactor, not a rewrite :: move the content that an agent does not need at run time into a separate references file cited as a secondary resource, and keep the write-time rules equivalent, so the round 2 comparison isolates the artefact cost rather than a rules change.
- Round 2 extends this record :: the intent (decide the mandate on benchmark evidence) is unchanged and the rubric discipline and artefacts folder carry over. Round 2 artefacts live under `round2/` in the same-stem folder.
  Rejected:
  - A follow-on record: it would split the decision evidence across two files.
- Round 2 samples exclude prose written under the incumbent mandate :: removes the incumbent's home-ground advantage that round 1 accepted. Vendored skill bodies (`skills/README.org` § Vendored) qualify by construction.
- Round 2 includes a no-skill, prompt-only control [2026-08-19] :: a skill costs context in every session that loads it, and no round so far has tested whether that cost buys anything over a one-line instruction. The control runs the same samples with no candidate skill available and one style line: "Use Simplified Technical English." Its artefact cost is zero loaded words, so it is the floor the four candidates must beat. A control that scores close to the best candidate is evidence that the mandate should be a prompt line rather than a skill.
  Note: the instruction as first proposed also opened with "Write docstrings, comments and test descriptions succinctly.", which names code artefacts that the prose samples do not contain. That sentence was dropped so the control measures only the instruction that applies to these samples. Restore it if a later round adds a code sample.
- Cost is measured in tokens, not proxied by loaded words [2026-08-19] :: every earlier round compared artefact cost in loaded words. `cost-analysis.md` extracts actual usage from the retained round 2 and round 3 transcripts, with a parse control and an arithmetic control that reconciles the summed fields against the reported totals. Per session, for identical work, the no-skill control costs 38% of the incumbent's tokens on `claude-sonnet-5` (37,824 against 98,566, and $0.16 against $0.48) and 30% on the local model (18,399 against 61,995). `simple-prose` is the cheapest adoptable skill in both rounds. `technical-prose` and the incumbent land within 0.15% of each other on the local model, which independently confirms that the refactor did not reduce what a session loads.
  The asymmetry this exposes: measured quality margins are 1 point, thin, and unreplicated, while measured cost differences are 1.8x to 3.4x, stable across two model tiers, two harnesses, and both paid and local runtimes, and recurring on every invocation for as long as a mandate stands.
- Round 3 repeats the five conditions on a limited local model [2026-08-19] :: round 2 ran on `claude-sonnet-5` and found every skill tied the no-skill control on strict text, with a thin one-point margin on explanatory prose. A capable model may simply not need the rules that a weaker model does, so a mandate decision taken from one strong model can be wrong for the models the mandate actually governs. Round 3 therefore re-runs all five conditions on `Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF-Q4_K_M` through pi's `lemonade` provider on the `strix` host, with the same samples and the same rubric. The user has already validated the model and harness.
  Hypothesis under test: the value of a prose skill is model-dependent. If the no-skill control loses clearly to the skills on the local model while tying on `claude-sonnet-5`, the skills earn their cost for weaker models and a no-skill mandate is wrong. If the control ties again, option 2 gains the support of a second, independent model tier.
  The comparison is within round 3. Round 3 scores are not compared against round 2 or round 1 scores, because the model differs in each round.
- Two-skill merge is not decidable from round 2 scores :: the benchmark decides the mandate, including a split by prose class; merging `technical-prose` and `simple-prose` depends on measured body overlap and routing-trigger clarity, known only after both skills exist. Recorded as an open question with those inputs.

** Risks
- Round 1 samples were written under the incumbent mandate, so the incumbent had home ground. Accepted for round 1; removed for round 2 by the sample-selection constraint.
- The gist can drift from the live gov.uk guidance. Mitigated: the `simple-prose` task cites the two gov.uk pages as the source of truth and uses the gist as prior art only.
- The mandate change amends the constitution (`home/AGENTS.md`). Recorded deliberately; the anti-criterion blocks any permanent change before the round 2 decision lands.
- `technical-prose` inherits the incumbent's rules, and the strict-text rubric dimension is STE-derived, so that pairing is structurally favoured. Accepted: the per-class dimension split from round 1 keeps the explanatory comparison independent.
- In-repo strict operational text that the mandate never touched may be scarce. Mitigated: vendored skill bodies are upstream text by construction; the staging task records provenance evidence per sample.
- One run per candidate remains the MVP bar, so a single defect can decide a score. Accepted, with an escape: the round 2 decide task may order a targeted rerun when the decision hinges on one unreplicated defect.
- Round 1 ran on `claude -p` because headless `pi --print` was refused, which round 1 misattributed to an account-level block. Resolved [2026-08-19]: the cause was `--no-extensions`, and `pi --print` is the verified round 2 harness (`round2/harness.md`). Round 2 scores still stand alone and are not compared with round 1 scores, because the harness and model differ between rounds.
- An error message can name a cause that is not the cause. The third-party usage text above described billing while the real fault was a disabled provider extension, and round 1 carried that misreading into a documented plan assumption. Isolate a headless session's tool surface with `--tools <allowlist>` rather than `--no-extensions`, and probe a refusal in a paired invocation before attributing it to an account or a provider.
- A split mandate makes agents route between two skills by prose class. Mitigated: the rewire task must state the routing rule; the routing-clarity assessment feeds the merge open question.
- The artefact-cost dimension can no longer separate `technical-prose` from the incumbent, because the refactor moved only ~93 words out of the loaded body (2,342 against 2,435). Accepted: round 2 rests on output quality, and the dimension still separates the lean artefacts from the two STE bodies.
- Withdrawn [2026-08-19]: a parent estimate that `technical-prose` reaches ~97% runtime-relevant density against the incumbent's ~84%. Round 2 measured ~84% for both, materially unchanged, because the 124-word opening framing paragraph survived the refactor almost intact against the incumbent's 125-word equivalent. The estimate adopted the refactoring child's self-reported residual of ~15 words instead of measuring the paragraph, and it was written into this record as a figure rather than as an estimate. Measure density at rule granularity before recording it.
